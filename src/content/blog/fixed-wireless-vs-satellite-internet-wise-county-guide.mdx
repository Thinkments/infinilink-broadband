## Understanding the Physics: Earth vs. Orbit

The fundamental difference between fixed wireless and satellite internet comes down to the distance data packets must travel.

### How Satellite Works
Traditional geostationary satellites orbit approximately 22,236 miles above Earth's equator. Even low-Earth-orbit (LEO) constellations orbit hundreds of miles in space. When you click a link, your signal must travel from your dish into space, back to a ground earth station, across the internet backbone, and back through space to your dish. This astronomical round trip creates severe latency (ping times often exceeding 60ms to 650ms).

### How Fixed Wireless Operates
Fixed wireless utilizes land-based communication towers positioned on high-elevation topography throughout Wise County. A compact, unobtrusive transceiver mounted on your roof maintains a direct line-of-sight microwave link with our local tower. The signal travels just 3 to 10 miles through the air at the speed of light, yielding ultra-responsive ping times of **15ms to 28ms**.

## Latency and Real-Time Performance: Gaming & Zoom

Bandwidth (Mbps) is only one part of the speed equation. Latency is what dictates how responsive your connection feels:

- **Online Multiplayer Gaming:** Titles like Call of Duty, Fortnite, and Rocket League require latency under 50ms. Fixed wireless easily handles this, whereas high satellite latency makes online multiplayer virtually unplayable.
- **Remote Work & Video Conferencing:** Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and Google Meet demand steady two-way packet streams. Satellite lag causes awkward pauses and dropped audio packets.
- **Smart Home & Security Cameras:** Constant upload streams from security cameras function seamlessly over low-latency fixed wireless links.

## Weather Resistance on Texas Ranches

A frequent question from Wise County homeowners is whether severe North Texas weather disrupts fixed wireless signals. Because Infinilink's point-to-multipoint microwave frequencies operate over short ground-level distances, thick storm clouds, heavy cloud cover, and rainstorms do not degrade the link compared to signals attempting to punch through the ionosphere into space.
